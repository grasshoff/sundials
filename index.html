Ancient Sundials.
Read from the stone.

A conic sundial turns the sun's path into a shadow on stone. The Berlin register holds 782 dialface entries of ancient sundials; 113 objects are measured on the 3D model and graded, and for 72 of them the latitude the dial was designed for can be read from the engraved lines. The figure shows how: choose a dial, a day and an hour, and watch the shadow of the nodus move on the hour network.

Gerd Graßhoff, Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin, Forschungsinstitut des Deutschen Museums

Methodological approach

Famulus

Every number on this page is read from the register, the PDAI collection or the papers when the page is built. If a source is missing, the build stops.

The register judges only what was measured. A fragment is not downgraded because parts are missing; the grade drops only for defects of the measurement itself. This page follows that rule.

What the latitude means for the day

For each of the 72 dials with a determined latitude the register computes the sun's noon altitude, the day length and the length of a seasonal hour at the solstices and the equinox, with the obliquity of the ecliptic of the year −100 (23.71°). Choose the quantity; each dot is one object, the lines are the geometric curves for comparison. A click opens the object page.

The latitude is the latitude the dial was designed for, not that of its find-spot. A dial may have been made for another klima than the one where it was found. ObjID 128 is a find from Delos, yet the register assigns its latitude of 43.61° to the klima of Massalia.

The papers — supplements to the repository

Seven papers are the supplements S1 to S7 of the repository. They carry the derivations behind the figures of this page: construction geometry, feature detection, model fitting, two worked examples and the method. Each entry says which claim it supports. All are drafts in alpha version and unpublished.

  1. S1

    Reconstructing Ancient Conic Sundials from 3D Scans

    A Research Programme in Construction Geometry, Measurement, and Literate Coding

    Supports: programme behind the measurement panel and astronomical picture · 7 pages

    Abstract

    The research task is to recover, from photogrammetric models of ancient conic sundials, the construction proportions in which the instruments were designed. This paper states the programme, reviews the June 2026 drafts on the research volume, and records the governing correction: the three seasonal meridian marks determine the cone and do not determine the latitude.

  2. S2

    Construction Geometry of the Conic Sundial with Horizontal Gnomon

    An Analytical Framework for Shadow Projection and Apex-Free Parameter Recovery on an Inclined Cone

    Supports: shadow animation and astronomical picture · 15 pages

    Abstract

    A conic sundial with its nodus on the polar axis is determined by three construction choices: the klima, the opening of the cone, and a scale. The three seasonal meridian marks determine the cone independently of latitude. Latitude is recovered from the hour division of the solstitial circles or from the dressed planes. A closed-form marking ratio Q recovers the half-angle without the apex being identified.

  3. S3

    Automated Feature Detection on Ancient Conic Sundial Fragments

    From Groove Morphology to Geometric Primitives via Cross-Sectional Profile Analysis

    Supports: detected hour and day lines on object pages · 5 pages

    Abstract

    A three-level hierarchical framework for automated feature perception on stone dialfaces: physical groove cut, medial centreline coordinate across sliced cross-sectional profiles, and geometric primitive fitting. Evaluated on ObjID 18 (BM inv. 2547) and ObjID 126 (Delos).

  4. S4

    Automated Model Optimization for Sundial Feature Detectors

    Nonlinear Parameter Estimation for Conic Sundial Reconstruction from 3D Scan Data

    Supports: cone fit and latitude estimates of the measurement panel · 5 pages

    Abstract

    Nonlinear parameter estimation methods for recovering the 7-parameter cone model from 3D scan data following Bard's estimation framework. Compares Nelder-Mead, TRF, and L-BFGS-B, resolving partial-arc degeneracy via coaxial constrained estimation.

  5. S5

    Geometrical Measurement of an Ancient Conic Sundial from a 3D Surface Model

    Construction Witnesses, Consensus Estimation, and the Proportions of Dialface ObjID 18 (British Museum inv. 2547)

    Supports: worked example, object page ObjID 18 · 10 pages

    Abstract

    Empirical deduction of construction proportions from the 3D surface model of Dialface ObjID 18 (BM inv. 2547). Evaluates four independent construction witnesses, calibrating the engraved angular unit and establishing the consensus latitude phi = 41.45 deg (Rome/Hellespont klima) with workshop rule x = 1:4 (12:15:20).

  6. S6

    The Cone Geometry of ObjID 126 (Delos): Latitude from the Engraved Network

    A Geometrically Explicit Estimation with Stated Assumptions and Defendable Confidence Intervals

    Supports: worked example, object page ObjID 126 · 6 pages

    Abstract

    Analysis of the 3D model of Dialface ObjID 126 from the Archaeological Museum of Delos. Isolates a +5.27% angular unit distortion via the equinoctial invariant HE = 90 deg, recovering the intended design latitude phi = 38.01 deg (Delos/Athens klima).

  7. S7

    Literate Coding with AI Agents: A Methodology for Computational Analysis of Scientific Objects

    Complexity Reduction through Structured Context, Executable Specification, and Closed-Loop Refinement

    Supports: how the measurement pipeline was built and checked · 5 pages

    Abstract

    A research methodology unifying Knuth's literate programming with autonomous AI agent capabilities. Employs a 3-level operational model (living reference background, structured planning substrate, agentic action) in a closed feedback loop on 3D sundial reconstruction.

Open points

  1. Latitude and cone: For 28 of the 72 latitudes the cone fitted to the day circles leaves a residual above 2 mm; their cone angle is withheld and the latitude shown as provisional (issue SD-30, open). ObjID 18 is the clearest case: 8.87 mm against 0.39 to 0.65 mm for four control cones. Until this is settled, ObjID 18 counts as cone preferred, not decisive.
  2. Units: 782 dialface entries belong to 694 objects. 9 of the 243 measurement records belong to objects that are not in the catalogue export.
  3. Places: 379 entries have no place coordinate. The field “Geolocation Site” may name the find-spot or another place; the holder is a separate field.
  4. ObjID 126: paper S6 gives a latitude of 38.01°, the register 37.97°. The two values are not reconciled.
  5. Below 25°: 6 latitudes lie below 25°; 0 of them is a consensus without the provisional flag. Read such values as flags of the measurement, not as findings about the dial.
  6. Spheres: The measurement pipeline is cone-only so far. For spherical objects (23 decisive) cone quantities such as the opening angle are not meaningful and are not shown; a latitude from hour angles holds for any surface of revolution about the polar axis (issue SD-29, open).
  7. Previews: Some point-selection previews of the repository are missing from the archive and return 404 (issue SD-27, open).
  8. Rights: The rights of the literature digitisations and of the engravings are not cleared.
  9. The papers are drafts in alpha version and unpublished.
